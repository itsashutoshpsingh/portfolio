01 / The job

What a security team needs on day one,
and what I bring.

The job, honestly

  • A queue of alerts, most of them noise, a few that matter.
  • A vulnerability backlog longer than anyone can fix this quarter.
  • Engineers who need the finding explained, and managers who need the risk explained.
  • Tooling that nobody has fully tuned.
  • Evidence that has to hold up when someone checks it: a timestamp, a cluster, a log.

What I have done already

  • Written Sigma and Splunk rules mapped to ATT&CK and tested them until they fire on the attack and stay quiet on normal activity.
  • Ranked a 1,000+ device estate's findings by CVSS so the riskiest were fixed first.
  • Sat with firmware engineers on fixes down to the bootloader, and wrote the remediation guidance management read.
  • Automated the routine scanning in Python because it was the boring part.
  • Measured how often Ethereum clustering rules and forensic timeline tools get it wrong, and published every miss.

05 / Method

How I work, in three steps.

  1. 01

    Find it

    Scan, read the firmware, walk the attack path yourself. Nessus and Qualys first, then hands on the hardware.

  2. 02

    Rank it and fix it with the engineers

    CVSS gives the number. Exposure, what it protects and the cost of the fix decide the order. Then sit with the people who own the code.

  3. 03

    Catch the rest

    Write the detection, map it to ATT&CK, test it against the lab. Automate whatever was boring.
