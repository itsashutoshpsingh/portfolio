What a SOC needs on day one,
and what I bring.

The job, honestly

  • A queue of alerts, most of them noise, a few that matter.
  • A vulnerability backlog longer than anyone can fix this quarter.
  • Engineers who need the finding explained, and managers who need the risk explained.
  • Tooling that nobody has fully tuned.

What I have done already

  • Written Sigma and Splunk rules mapped to ATT&CK and tested them until they fire on the attack and stay quiet on normal activity.
  • Ranked a 1,000+ device estate's findings by CVSS so the riskiest were fixed first.

The story

  1. 2020, Pune

    A computer science degree, and a question

    I started a BTech in Computer Science Engineering at Symbiosis Institute of Technology. The question that stuck was not how systems work but how they fail, and who notices first.

  2. 2022, Verzeo

    First penetration tests

    Ten-plus Windows targets with Metasploit and Nmap on Kali Linux. OWASP-aligned findings reports. Controlled phishing simulations with the Social-Engineer Toolkit, and the counter-strategies that followed. I learned to write a finding someone else could act on.

    • Kali Linux
    • Nmap
    • Metasploit
    • SET
    • OWASP Top 10

  3. 2023, Cisco AICTE programme

    Build the network, then break it

    I designed and simulated a 50+ node multi-site network in Packet Tracer, with routing, VLAN segmentation, firewall and ACL configuration. Then I audited my own design, found 15+ weaknesses, ranked the mitigations by risk, implemented IAM and RBAC for data-access governance, and built a Packet Flow Visualiser to see traffic move.

    • TCP/IP
    • VLANs
    • ACLs
    • IAM
    • RBAC

  4. 2024, NASSCOM and Tutelr Infosec

    Policy by day, labs by night

    At NASSCOM, India's national IT industry body, I led synthetic-media governance research: 250+ detection and provenance tools surveyed, hands-on with C2PA, Google SynthID and Meta Stable Signature, mapped to the Online Safety Act and the EU AI Act. I co-authored a policy document for senior government and industry stakeholders and presented to 100+ professionals from government, Adobe, Meta and Google at a closed-door event. The research became a peer-reviewed paper in IJFMR.

    In the evenings I completed Tutelr Infosec's industry programme with Palo Alto Networks, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Mandiant: 20+ labs across cloud security, DFIR, penetration testing and blue-team operations, producing incident-response playbooks, evidence-acquisition reports and cloud misconfiguration assessments.

    • C2PA
    • SynthID
    • EU AI Act
    • DFIR
    • Playbooks
    • Google Cybersecurity Certificate

  5. 2024, Kyoka Embedded Systems

    Seven months on production devices

    A smart-home IoT estate of 1,000+ connected switches and fan controllers. I ran Nessus and Qualys assessments across it, prioritised critical findings by CVSS and remediated through firmware hardening. Hardware and firmware analysis turned up five bootloader-level flaws, and I worked with engineering on each fix. I automated the routine scanning in Python, supported the rollout of mutual TLS with hardware-backed key storage, wrote the reports management read, and kept the security monitoring running day to day.

    • Nessus
    • Qualys
    • CVSS
    • Firmware analysis
    • mTLS
    • Python

  6. 2025, University of Warwick

    The UK, and an NCSC-certified MSc

    I moved to Coventry for the MSc in Cyber Security Engineering at WMG, with AI for Cyber Security as my elective. The ethical hacking module ended in a full penetration test of a simulated corporate network and a professional report. The AI module compared Random Forest and XGBoost on CIC-IDS-2017, and XGBoost won on the metric a SOC cares about: fewer missed attacks. My dissertation, PrivDeFi, used federated graph neural networks with differential privacy to flag fraud on Ethereum and BNB Smart Chain, mapped to MiCA and DORA.

    • Penetration testing
    • XGBoost
    • Graph neural networks
    • Differential privacy

  7. 2026, Coventry

    The lab, the shop floor, and the search

    The MSc is done. In my home lab, Windows telemetry flows through Sysmon into Elastic, and I write Sigma and Splunk rules mapped to ATT&CK and test each one against it. On Hack The Box and TryHackMe I practise the attacker's side: Kerberoasting, BloodHound, Impacket, Evil-WinRM, Burp Suite and sqlmap. The SC-200 exam is booked.

    Alongside the search I work part time in retail in Coventry. It is customer-facing, fast and unglamorous, and it keeps the habits a SOC runs on sharp: showing up reliably, staying calm when the queue builds, and explaining things clearly to people who do not share your vocabulary.

    • Elastic
    • Sysmon

    • Splunk

Firmware was the fun part. The scanning wasn't, so I automated it. Now I build the detection side: rules that fire on the attack and stay quiet on normal activity.

How I work, in three steps.

  1. 01

    Find it

    Scan, read the firmware, walk the attack path yourself. Nessus and Qualys first, then hands on the hardware.

  2. 02

    Rank it and fix it with the engineers

    CVSS gives the number. Exposure, what it protects and the cost of the fix decide the order. Then sit with the people who own the code.

  3. 03

    Catch the rest

    Write the detection, map it to ATT&CK, test it against the lab. Automate whatever was boring.
